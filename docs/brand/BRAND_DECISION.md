# Brand Decision Document — AI Personal Performance Coach

**Status:** Decision document, v1  
**Date:** 2026-10-06  
**Scope:** Replaces the working name PREP with a new brand. Covers name, tagline system, positioning, personality, voice, feature naming, visual direction and app-icon direction. Does not cover UI design.

> Legal note: no trademark clearance has been performed. The conflict notes below come from web searches on 2026-10-06 and are a first screen only. Domain registries (RDAP/WHOIS) were not reachable from this environment, so domain availability is unverified. A professional trademark search in classes 9, 41, 42 and 44 (EU, UK, US) plus App Store name checks are required before launch.

---

## 1. The problem the brand has to solve

The product is not a tracker, a calorie counter, a workout library, a dashboard or a chatbot. It is a coach that listens to what is happening in your life and changes the plan: training, sport, nutrition, recovery, body composition, wearable data and daily planning, held together by one reasoning layer.

The category is crowded with products that *measure*. Almost none *decide*. The brand therefore has one job: make "adaptive coaching" feel premium, athletic and human, and make "tracking" feel like the old world.

Everything below follows from one sentence:

**You tell it what is happening. It changes the plan.**

### 2.4 Five finalists, assessed

**TARMO**

- Sound: two open syllables, hard T, rolling R, closed M-O. Grounded and confident. Pronounced identically in English, Dutch, German, Spanish, Italian, French and the Nordics.
- Memorability: high. Unusual letter pattern, no close English word.
- International: no negative meanings found in major European languages. In Spanish it loosely echoes "tramo" (a stretch of road), which is neutral.
- Premium feel: Nordic. It sits naturally next to Polar, Suunto and Oura, the Finnish lineage of sports technology, without copying any of them.
- Spelling: phonetic, no doubled letters, no silent letters.
- App icon: strong initial T; the word fits a 5-character wordmark.
- Category flexibility: carries no sport, no fitness, no AI. Works for a company, an app, a hardware line, a coaching service.
- Negative associations: it is a common male first name in Finland and Estonia (roughly like "Henk" in the Netherlands). Irrelevant outside those markets; in Finland it will read as a person's name, which is survivable. Weak echo of "tarmac".
- Obvious competitors with similar names: none in fitness, sport or health found.
- Domains and social: unverified (registry lookups blocked). Expect tarmo.com to be held; plan for tarmo.app, tarmo.coach or a brand-plus-word domain. "tarmo" handles likely need a suffix.
- Trademark risk: **medium**. An EU trademark TARMO (no. 016868457, Tammer Brands Oy, Finland, 2017) exists for a hardware and tools brand across many classes. Whether it covers class 9 (software), 41 (training) or 42 (SaaS) must be checked by counsel. A Finnish property-management app "Tarmo Mobiili" also exists in a different category.

**TAKT**

- Sound: one syllable, two hard consonants. Crisp, minimal, decisive.
- Memorability: very high. Four letters.
- International: German, Danish, Swedish and Norwegian word for beat, rhythm and bar; also tact in German and the Nordics. English readers see "tact". Dutch readers recognise it via German.
- Premium feel: very high; Scandinavian design register.
- Spelling: English speakers may write "tact" or "tacked".
- App icon: exceptional; a four-letter wordmark or a metronome-like mark.
- Category flexibility: high.
- Negative associations: "takt time" (lean manufacturing) for operations people. Slightly cold.
- Competitors and conflicts: **high**. The App Store already lists at least six apps named Takt (focus timer, task tracker, time tracker, metronome, takt-time calculator) and one consumer AI assistant, "Takt – an AI with taste" by Takt LLC. TAKT A/S (Danish furniture) holds registered marks. A warehouse-software company also uses the name.
- Verdict: best brand on paper, worst clearance picture. Not usable as the primary name without a fight.

**TAHTI**

- Sound: soft, light, double-T. Pleasant.
- Meaning: Finnish for pace, tempo, beat; with an umlaut (tähti) it means star. Very close to the product idea of pacing a day.
- International: easy to say; English readers may hear "Tahiti", which pulls the brand toward holiday imagery.
- Premium feel: good, but lighter and softer than the brief's "athletic, confident".
- Spelling: "tahti" versus "tatchi" or "tati" errors likely.
- App icon: T; same as Tarmo.
- Conflicts: none found in fitness, sport or health. TAHTO (one letter away) is a Finnish sports museum and two IT companies.
- Trademark risk: low to medium (unverified).
- Verdict: cleanest of the finalists, slightly too gentle for the brand position.

**APLOMB**

- Meaning: composure, self-assurance. English and French; "aplomo" in Spanish, "aplomb" used in Dutch, German and Italian. This is exactly the coach's personality.
- Sound: a-PLOM. Silent b trips some readers.
- Premium feel: high, elegant, slightly old-world.
- Athletic feel: weak. It is a word for a diplomat more than for an athlete.
- Negative associations: "plomb" is lead (the metal) in French; "a plum" in English.
- Conflicts: Aplomb Technologies (two IT consultancies), an Avon mark (expired), recent US and EU filings in beverages and cosmetics. None in fitness.
- Verdict: a strong secondary-brand word (a voice principle, a tier name), not the company name.

**VELTRO**

- Meaning: in Dante, the swift hound prophesied to bring order; Italian for a greyhound. Speed, lean grace, direction.
- Sound: VEL-tro. Easy.
- Premium feel: good, Italian.
- Negative associations: **sounds like Velcro.** That alone disqualifies it as a premium name.
- Conflicts: EU mark VELTRO held by Ravago S.A. (materials), several small software agencies, a shoe-fit app, a watch face. None in fitness.
- Verdict: out on the Velcro echo.

### 2.5 Three strongest

1. **Tarmo** — best balance of sound, meaning, premium feel and clearance picture.
2. **Takt** — best pure brand, blocked by App Store and trademark crowding.
3. **Tahti** — cleanest clearance, softest character.

### 2.6 Recommended winner: TARMO

Why Tarmo wins:

- It names the thing the product gives you that trackers do not: the drive to act on what your body is telling you. "Tarmo" is the energy to go, the get-up-and-go, in a language with real sports-science credibility.
- It is abstract everywhere except Finland, so it can mean whatever the brand makes it mean.
- It sounds like a company, not a feature. "Tarmo Oy", "Tarmo Labs", "Tarmo for teams" all work.
- It has a T that can carry an icon and five letters that fit a wordmark on a watch face.
- It passes the "say it across a padel court" test and the "say it to an investor" test.

Conditions:

- Counsel must check EUTM 016868457 (Tammer Brands) class coverage. If class 9 or 42 is covered and in use, the fallback order is Tahti, then a reconsideration of Takt with a distinguishing wordmark.
- Secure the .app and .coach domains plus a brand-plus-word .com (for example gettarmo.com, tarmo.coach) before any public use.

How to introduce it: "Tarmo (TAR-mo) is the Finnish word for the drive to act. It is what a good coach gives you." Always written as **Tarmo** in prose and **TARMO** in the wordmark. Never "Tarmo AI", "Tarmo Coach" or "MyTarmo".

## 3. Tagline system

Rule for every line: it must be false for a tracker, a calorie counter, a workout library and a chatbot. If a competitor could say it, it is not ours.

**Brand tagline**

> **Coached, not tracked.**

It draws the category line in three words. It is true of the product, false of the wearables and loggers, and it invites the question "what is the difference", which the product then answers.

**Product descriptor** (used under the name in stores, press, decks)

> Tarmo is an adaptive performance coach for training, sport, nutrition and recovery.

**App Store subtitle** (30-character limit)

> Adaptive performance coach

**App Store promotional line**

> Tell Tarmo what is happening. Your training, meals and recovery change with it.

**Short elevator pitch**

> Tarmo is a personal performance coach that adapts to your real life. You tell it what is happening — "35 minutes, stiff knee, padel tonight" — and it reshapes your session, your meals and your recovery for that day, explains what changed, and remembers. Trackers measure you. Tarmo coaches you.

**Supporting lines** (campaigns, onboarding, empty states; never all at once)

- Tell it what is happening.
- It changes when your day does.
- Built for the day you actually have.
- One coach. Every variable.
- Less logging. More coaching.

Retired for good: "Be your best", "Unlock your potential", "Train smarter", "AI-powered", "Crush", "Smash", "Beast".

## 4. Positioning

**Statement**

For people who train, play a sport and still have a life, Tarmo is the personal performance coach that adapts training, nutrition and recovery to the day you actually have, because it listens, reasons and changes the plan instead of only recording what you did.

**Who it is for**

The serious amateur. They play padel on Tuesday and Thursday, lift twice a week, want to lose a little fat or add a little muscle, own a watch or a ring, and are tired of five apps that do not talk to each other. Later: runners, cyclists, tennis and football players, anyone balancing a sport with a gym and a job.

**Who it is not for**

Elite athletes with a human staff. Casual step-counters. Biohackers who want 40 biomarkers on one screen.

**Against the alternatives**

| Alternative | What it does | What Tarmo does instead |
|---|---|---|
| Wearable apps (ring, strap, watch) | Measure recovery and strain, then leave the decision to you | Takes the decision and reshapes the day |
| Calorie and macro loggers | Count what you ate | Move the pre-match meal and adjust targets when the session changes |
| Workout libraries and plan apps | Hand you a fixed programme | Rewrite today's session around time, pain and tonight's match |
| General AI chat | Answer questions from zero context | Remember you, read your data, and act in the app |

**Brand universe**

Somewhere between a private training club, modern performance science, and the best consumer hardware. Think the calm of a well-run physio room and the energy of a floodlit court at 21:00, not a gym floor and not a laboratory.

## 5. Brand personality

Tarmo behaves like an elite coach who has seen a thousand athletes and does not need to prove it.

| Trait | What it means in product | What it rules out |
|---|---|---|
| Calm | Short sentences. One decision at a time. Never a wall of alerts. | Exclamation marks, confetti, streak panic |
| Decisive | Says what it changed and why in one breath. | "You might want to consider…" |
| Observant | Notices patterns before you ask: "Third week your Thursday HRV dips after Wednesday padel." | Generic tips |
| Supportive | Backs you on the hard days without cheerleading. | "Great job!" after logging water |
| Direct | Tells you to skip the heavy squats tonight. | Hedging, disclaimers in every message |
| Intelligent | Uses your data and your words together. | Quoting a study at you |
| Adaptive | Changes the plan, then moves on. | Making you edit five screens |

The coach is a person with a point of view, not a mascot and not a chatbot. It has no name other than Tarmo and no face.

## 6. Voice and tone

**Principles**

1. Lead with the decision, follow with the reason. "Lower-body volume cut by a third. You play at 20:00."
2. One message, one point. If there are three changes, show a list, not three paragraphs.
3. Plain words for the body. "Stiff knee", not "patellofemoral discomfort". "Fuel", not "macronutrient distribution".
4. Numbers only when they change what the person does. "Eat 40 g more carbs at lunch" is useful. "Your readiness is 73" is only useful with a decision attached.
5. Praise is rare and specific. "Four sessions this week with a sore knee and no missed padel. That is discipline." Never praise for opening the app.
6. Admit limits in one line. "I can't see your sleep from last night. Tell me roughly, or I'll assume average."
7. Never shout. No exclamation marks in coach messages. Marketing may use one per page.

**Examples**

Good:
> Your recovery is strong, but you have padel tonight. I've shortened the lower-body work so you keep your explosiveness.

Good:
> 35 minutes, stiff knee, match tonight. Here is the session: upper-body push and pull, light hip mobility, no plyometrics. Lunch gets 40 g more carbs. Recovery tonight is a 10-minute walk, not foam rolling.

Bad:
> Awesome job! You're crushing it! Let's smash today's workout! 💪🔥

Bad:
> Based on your HRV, RHR and sleep architecture, the algorithm recommends a modified training stimulus.

**Banned words in product copy**: crush, smash, beast, grind, hustle, journey, unlock, unleash, supercharge, AI-powered, algorithm, optimise (as a verb to the user), amazing, awesome.

**Coach messages are set apart visually** (see section 8) so the user always knows when Tarmo is speaking versus when the interface is labelling.

## 7. Brand architecture and feature naming

**Rule:** plain words. A feature gets a special name only when the plain word would be wrong or ambiguous. No trademark symbols on features. Verbs for the things you do, nouns for the things you read.

| Concept | Name | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| The product and the coach | **Tarmo** | The coach is not a separate character. "Ask Tarmo", "Tell Tarmo". |

| Nutrition | **Fuel** | Targets, meals, timing. Not "Diet". |
| Recovery, sleep, mobility | **Recover** | |

## 8. Visual world (strategic direction, not screens)

**Visual personality in one line**

A floodlit court at night: dark, warm, precise, with one colour that means "pay attention".

**Light versus dark**

Dark-default in the product, with a complete light theme. The dark is a warm graphite, never pure black and never blue-black. The light theme is bone, not white. Marketing uses both so the brand is recognisable in either.

**Colour philosophy**

- Base: warm graphite scale (five steps) and a bone scale (five steps). Neutrals carry 90 percent of every screen.
- One accent: **sodium amber**, the colour of stadium light on a dark sky. Used only for the coach's actions and for "what changed". It must never fill backgrounds, buttons by default, or chart areas.
- Semantic colours for readiness states are muted versions of green, amber and red, desaturated to sit inside the neutral scale. No traffic-light brightness.
- No neon green (wearable territory), no electric purple (AI-gimmick territory), no orange-red (Strava territory).

**Typography**

- Display: a geometric grotesk with slightly condensed numerals and tight tracking, used large for the few numbers that matter. Numbers are the hero of this brand; type does the work that imagery does elsewhere.
- Text: a humanist sans with excellent small sizes and tabular figures.
- Coach voice: the text face, set with a thin amber rule on the left and a slightly larger size, so a coach message is recognisable without a label or avatar.
- No italics as a "sport" effect. No all-caps shouting outside small labels.

**Iconography**

Line icons, single stroke weight, rounded terminals, monochrome. Icons label, they do not decorate. No filled multi-colour icons. No emoji in the product.

**Photography and imagery**

Real people in real light, late in the day: a court at dusk, a kitchen counter at 07:10, a hallway with a bag by the door. Motion is allowed to blur. Show the sport, the gym and the life in the same frame. Diverse ages, bodies and sports. No six-pack close-ups, no stock gym floors, no neon-lit "performance labs", no beauty-retouching.

**Motion**

Decisive and short. 150 to 250 ms, ease-out, no bounce. When the plan changes, items re-sort and the changed ones hold a brief amber highlight that fades. Coach messages arrive whole, not typed out letter by letter. Nothing pulses to get attention.

**Charts**

Line-first, one series in neutral, one in amber when comparing. No gridlines heavier than the data. No 3D, no gradients under lines, no radial gauges with needles. Readiness is a single horizontal bar with a marker, not a ring. Sparklines everywhere a trend helps, full charts only in Progress.

**Gradients**

None as decoration. Tonal depth is allowed within 3 percent luminance for surface separation. The app icon may use one controlled gradient.

**Glass and translucency**

Not a visual theme. One permitted use: the floating check-in bar over scrolling content.

**Radius**

One system: 16 px for cards, 12 px for inputs and buttons, full round for chips and the check-in bar. No sharp corners, no mixed radii on one screen.

**Density and whitespace**

Medium-high. Athletes want information. Generous space at the top of Today around the decision of the day; compact, aligned lists below. Whitespace is spent on hierarchy, not on emptiness.

**Illustration policy**

None in the product. Empty states use a short coach sentence, not a drawing. No mascots, no 3D blobs, no abstract swooshes. Marketing may use geometric diagrams of "the plan that moved".

**Recognisable without the logo**

Warm graphite, bone, one amber, large tabular numerals, the amber-ruled coach line, and the "What changed" strip. Those six things together are the brand.

## 9. App icon: three conceptual directions

All three are monochrome-plus-amber on warm graphite, with a light-theme variant on bone. Each must read at 16 px and in a grid next to neon fitness icons.

**Direction A — "Shift" (recommended)**

Two thick horizontal bars. The lower bar is offset to the right by one bar-height. It is the plan that moved: the straight line of what was planned and the adjusted line of what will happen. At small sizes it reads as a bold abstract mark; at large sizes the offset is clearly intentional. One bar in bone, the other in amber. This is the only icon in the category that depicts adaptation itself.

**Direction B — "Forward T"**

An exceptional letterform rather than an initial: a T whose crossbar extends only to the right of the stem, so the letter leans into motion without italics. Stem in bone, crossbar in amber. Reads as a T, a direction marker and a start line at once. Safe, legible, and still ownable because of the asymmetry.

**Direction C — "Floodlight"**

A single amber disc, slightly off-centre, with a soft falloff into the graphite, like a court light at night. No letter, no symbol. Extremely distinctive in a grid and the strongest expression of the visual world. Risk: without the wordmark it can be mistaken for a weather or sun app, so it depends on consistent marketing.

Recommendation: develop A and B to finished form, test on real home screens, ship A unless legibility testing says otherwise. Avoided by design: dumbbells, hearts, lightning bolts, brains, chat bubbles, rings.
